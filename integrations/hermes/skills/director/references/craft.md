# Craft, typography and color

Connect audience need to a visual idea, then to a distinguishing construction and
a feature that survives the stated display width. Different directions should vary
meaning, silhouette, letter skeleton, proportions, rhythm, counters or construction;
recoloring the same generic mark is not a distinct concept.

Keep a concise shared brand strategy: positioning, audience need, promise, a
distinguishing visual principle, typography and color roles. Explain the connection
between the supplied product and the visible decision. Three adjectives alone are
not a strategy. Treat missing details as assumptions, not researched market facts.
The native image request receives the six visual fields as bounded advisory
context, without the assumption log. Keep each field within 320 characters and
put the specific decision first. Long fields are visibly shortened with an ellipsis
to fit the existing image-prompt limit. Generation guidance and strategy form an
optional final section; if the original brief/direction fills that allowance, the
section is shortened or omitted. Edits omit this recognized generation-only section
while retaining the original brief, direction, parent pixels and current feedback.
The full saved strategy and parent prompt remain unchanged. Directions must also
express the chosen visual decisions. Exact
user text, colors, background, requested features and revision constraints take
precedence; planning must never silently rewrite the original brief.

Describe purposeful contours and any necessary joints or overlaps; do not add an
arbitrary cut to make an ordinary glyph seem original. Relate curves, corners, stroke
weights, terminals and gaps. Inspect optical centering, clear space and the dominant
shape before decorative effects. A rich title can be appropriate; minimalism and a
fixed number of colors are not universal quality requirements.

For lettering, preserve the exact source string character for character. Do not add
a slogan, romanize Hangul or turn syllables into pseudo-letters. Name the intended
counter openness, crowded pairs, stroke contrast and reading order. A wordmark is
lettering; a combination mark uses the requested symbol/text arrangement with clear
hierarchy and a readable gap. A named font suggests appearance only. Do not claim
that an actual font file was typeset, licensed, embedded or made editable.

Choose type for the brand's voice and actual language: skeleton, width, weight,
stroke contrast, terminals and spacing before a font name. Natural letter anatomy
and optical kerning come first. Preserve Hangul syllable structure and judge its
weight and rhythm alongside Latin text when mixed. Do not force every name into
sharp stencil cuts, geometric fusions or a futuristic custom alphabet. For a
combination mark, deliberately set the symbol-to-text scale, baseline relationship
and gap. A symbol-only request does not need ornamental typography added to it.

Assign colors to the dominant form, letters, accent, separators and background as
needed. Judge adjacent color separation and placement, not swatches in isolation.
Advisory colors are not verified exact palette compliance. Do not invent a maximum
unique raster-color count or require a monochrome variant unless requested.

Make the palette a system of relationships: a dominant role, quieter supporting
roles and a focal accent only where useful. Specify each role's relative area,
lightness and saturation; keep adjacent forms distinguishable at use size. A warm
and cool pair can work when one leads and the other supports. Several equally loud
accents often compete. Use fewer roles if an additional color has no job, without
turning that judgment into a hard color-count rule. A brand adjective alone does
not justify beige, navy-and-gold, neon gradients or an all-dark palette. User colors
remain authoritative visual intent; an advisory native render cannot certify exact
hex values or font/UI contrast compliance.

Use the requested display width for judging the mark. Inspect specific gaps, joins,
counters and reading order at that size, not only a large preview. A 360px title is
not automatically a 32px favicon. Require visible observations and concrete fixes;
insufficient evidence remains not_observed. Never infer popularity or market fit
from a familiar shape. Strategy is a proposed interpretation of the supplied brief.

For an app icon, identify one primary form before choosing its material. Signature
marks, compact exact lettering, tactile objects, organic emblems, characters and
modular geometry are valid when the product supports them. Do not combine all of
them in one direction. Do not automatically map education to an owl, health to a
leaf, habits to fire, or AI to a sparkle/orbit/infinity mark. Do not imitate a known
app's silhouette. Reference traits such as open counters, related radii or a single
light source are transferable; the distinctive identity of a reference is not.

Supply concise design specifications: dominant form, proportion/curve/contact or
lettering decisions, color roles, material/depth, reference traits and excluded
features, small-size invariant, structural difference and one concrete failure risk.
The spec is intent. Later critics must name visible locations and evidence, including
what disappears at 32/48/64/128px, without turning color counts or symmetry into taste
rules. These widths are diagnostics, not universal platform delivery requirements.
One-direction variations retain the primary idea and change explicit shape variables;
different directions require structural differences, not recoloring.

Layered form and glass appearance are different concerns. A painted glossy PNG has
no editable layers. Avoid a rounded tile nested inside a square tile. Do not infer
OS rendering, platform acceptance or global uniqueness from a raster preview.

For new brand artwork, build a usable identity first: a coherent silhouette or
readable wordmark whose distinguishing feature survives without presentation
effects. A shape need not illustrate the product literally. Do not fuse unrelated
symbols, invent a fictional industrial badge, or add cuts to signal innovation.
The default is flat original artwork. Cinematic lighting, brushed metal, embossing,
bevels, dramatic shine and game-faction styling need an explicit reason in the user
request; professional, premium and innovative do not imply those treatments.

For an opaque brand canvas, default to pure white #FFFFFF across the exterior.
An explicit exterior canvas color in the brief's notes/colors overrides this
default; transparent requests remain transparent. Avoid unrequested cream/beige,
paper texture, backdrop gradients, vignettes and exterior cast shadows. A requested
colored backplate is foreground artwork, not permission to tint surrounding space.

A later brand board can show how a selected identity works across applications,
but presentation cannot repair a weak mark. Keep exact original logo assets and
color values separate from generated mockups. Never pass a composite board off as
editable logo files or generate a board instead of the requested single original.

Save the exact final prompt. Request one original square artwork per call, with no
stock-logo collection, grid, contact sheet, staged mockup or device frame. For edits,
name the exact visible defect, requested change and features to retain from the
parent. Compare the child against that parent at the same display width and report
regressions. No automatic aesthetic reroll is part of this workflow.
